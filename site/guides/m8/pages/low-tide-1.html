Low Tide · step 1 of 10

The plan and the setup

Low Tide is slow and wide: 84 BPM in D minor, with a pad that swells and darkens like water going out, a round bass, swung drums, an arp you write by hand, and a sound you record wherever you are. It takes ten steps. This one shows where the harmony is going and sets up the song.

Press Play above to hear the chords as they'll be after step 3: the pad and the roots, nothing else yet.

Where we're going

Eight bars, two per chord except at the end, where the last chord splits in two. Three ideas do most of the work.

The floor moves, the room stays

D minor to B♭ major (i to ♭VI) is one of the most used moves in trip-hop and ambient music, and the reason is in the notes. Dm is D F A; B♭maj7 is B♭ D F A. They share three notes, so the pad barely changes while the bass falls a major third underneath it. In bar 3 the pad plays exactly the notes it played in bar 1, and the chord still changes because the root did.

Rootless chords

This is the same trick as Faded Polaroid: the pad never plays the root. It plays the upper structure, the notes above the root, and a separate track holds the root down low. F A C E over a D is Dm9. F A B♭ D over a G is Gm9. That keeps the pad floating and the bottom clean, and it means one pad shape can serve two chords.

A top voice that orbits one note

Follow the thick line in the roll. The pad's top note is D, rises to E in bar 2, comes back to D and stays there for five bars while the chords change underneath it. In bar 8 it slips down to C♯, the only C♯ in the loop and its one bright note, which pulls straight back to D at the top. At that moment every voice in the pad moves up a step at once (E to F, G to A, B♭ to C, C♯ to D), which is why the loop feels like it comes home.

Bar 3, where nothing in the pad moves but the chord changes anyway. Then the end of bar 8 into bar 1: the C♯ resolving.

Set up the song

  1. Save whatever you're working on. Then go to the Project view (it sits above the Song view; SHIFT+arrows move between views, and the mini-map at the bottom right shows where you are) and choose NEW.
  2. Set TEMPO to 084.
  3. Set NAME to LOW TIDE and SAVE.
  4. Leave SCALE alone. A scale would quantize notes to D minor, and the C♯ in bar 8 isn't in D minor: it would get bent to C or D, and the loop would lose its pull home.
  5. Leave GROOVE on the default for now. Swing arrives with the drums in step 5, and it can't be heard on chords that only move on row 0.
