Low Tide · step 1 of 10

The plan and the setup

Low Tide is slow and wide: 84 BPM in D minor, with a pad that swells and darkens like water going out, a round bass, swung drums, an arp you write by hand, and a sound you record wherever you are. It takes ten steps. This one shows where the harmony is going and sets up the song.

Press Play above to hear the chords as they'll be after step 3: the pad and the roots, nothing else yet.

Where we're going

Eight bars, two per root. The first and last roots each carry two voicings. Three ideas do most of the work.

The floor moves, the room stays

D minor to B♭ major (i to ♭VI) is one of the most used moves in trip-hop and ambient music, and the reason is in the notes. Dm9 is D F A C E; B♭maj9 is B♭ D F A C. They share four notes, so the pad barely changes while the bass falls a major third underneath it. Bar 3's pad is bar 1's with one note moved up a half step (E to F), and the chord still changes, because the root did.

Rootless chords on one held note

As in Faded Polaroid, the pad never plays the root. It plays the upper structure, the notes above the root, and a separate track holds the root down low. F C E A D over a D is Dm9. F B♭ F A D over a G is Gm9. That keeps the pad floating and the bottom clean.

The pad is also one note, held for the whole loop. You type D-3 once, and each chord is a chord bank: a list of intervals above that D. A command, CRD, switches banks on a row with no note, so the chord changes while the note keeps sounding. Nothing re-attacks; the pad just moves.

A top voice that orbits one note

Follow the thick line in the roll. The pad's top note is D, rises to E in bar 2, comes back to D and stays there for five bars while the chords change underneath it. In bar 8 it slips down to C♯, the only C♯ in the loop and its one bright note, which pulls straight back to D at the top. At that moment four voices step up at once (E to F, B♭ to C, G to A, C♯ to D) and one holds, which is why the loop feels like it comes home.

The voicings are open: no two neighboring notes closer than a minor third, most a fourth or more apart, from F3 up to D5. Each note in a Hypersynth chord is a pair of detuned saws, and saws packed a step apart beat against each other and turn to buzz. You heard that on your M8 in the first version of these chords. The other half of the fix is a much smaller detune, in step 2.

Bar 3, where one pad note moves and the whole chord changes anyway. Then the end of bar 8 into bar 1: the C♯ resolving.

Set up the song

  1. Save whatever you're working on. Then go to the Project view (it sits above the Song view; SHIFT+arrows move between views, and the mini-map at the bottom right shows where you are) and choose NEW.
  2. Set TEMPO to 084.
  3. Set NAME to LOW TIDE and SAVE.
  4. Leave SCALE alone. A scale would quantize notes to D minor, and the C♯ in bar 8 isn't in D minor: it would get bent to C or D, and the loop would lose its pull home.
  5. Leave GROOVE on the default. Swing arrives with the drums in step 5, on the drum and bass tracks only, so the pad stays straight and floats over it.
