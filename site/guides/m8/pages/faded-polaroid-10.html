Arrange and mix

Everything exists; now it becomes a song. Nine rows of eight bars: the memory alone, your loop, the beat, the hook, the fade, the hook again, a breakdown at the memory's speed, the hook's last time, and an outro that rewinds and freezes. Then the mix: one room, a shimmer on the lead's tail, and a limiter.

The silence chain: FE in every gap

On the M8 an empty song cell (--) doesn't mean silence: the track "will loop back to the beginning of its list of chains" (manual), out of step with the others. So every gap in the song above holds chain FE, which you made in step 9: an OFF, then seven empty bars. The parts sheet draws those cells empty; FE is how the M8 keeps them quiet. Nothing is left ringing into one anyway: the drums and bass decay on their own, and the lead and chords end with OFFs in their own chains.

Lay it out

  1. Your workbench rows 00 to 02 become the intro and A rows. First copy row 00 (your full loop) to rows 03, 05 and 07: on row 00, track 1, SHIFT+OPTION, move right to track 6, OPTION copies; on the target row, track 1, SHIFT+EDIT pastes. Row 01 (the B section) moves to row 04 the same way.
  2. Then fill every cell from the song screen above, row by row. Cells that read FE are the silence chain.
  3. Make the new chains below by cloning (SHIFT+OPTION then EDIT on a chain number copies it to a new one) and changing their rows.

The arp steps back: chain 11

Under the hook (rows 03, 05, 07), your arp keeps its notes and register but drops back: velocity 3C on the beat and 2C off it, and CHA 8F on rows 6 and E, which gives those notes a little over half a chance and always runs the FX. Phrases 14 to 17 are 10 to 13 with those changes. Not another octave: down, it muddies the chords; up, it runs into the lead.

The breakdown: everything at the memory's speed

Row 06 plays the memory (instrument 09 at V 40, chain 71) under your loop. At half speed the memory changes chord every two bars, so the live parts slow down to match: the arp plays each of your bars twice (chain 13) and the chords strike every other bar (chain 32). Otherwise bar 3 would put a live Bm9 over a remembered Aadd9. No bass, no drums.

The lead plays only the reach, once (chain 43): it starts in bar 2, straddling the slowed D-to-A change, and leaves B5 hanging. Its first note carries TBL 04, back to the lead's own table after table 81 in row 05.

The resolution: chains 44 and 45

Row 07 is the hook as in row 03, except its last bar (phrase 49): B5, A5, then up to E6 on beat 3, held. Over Gmaj9 that E is the thirteenth, the one note of the hook outside its chord. The outro's first downbeat steps it down to D6 (chain 45, phrase 4E), over Dmaj9 and a D2 bass (chain 25): the root, the first and only time.

The outro: rewinding, then a still picture

  • Chords, chain 33: Dmaj9 struck in bar 1 and again, softer, in bar 3 (phrase 3D, V 40), then an OFF in bar 5 (phrase 3E). The two-bar AHD has faded by then; the OFF is a safety.
  • The memory backward, chain 72: instrument 0A on C-3. Played backward, your progression runs in reverse, two bars a chord: Gmaj9, Bm9, Aadd9, Dmaj9.
  • The freeze, chain 81 on track 8: XRZ FF on row 8 of the last bar. XRZ freezes the reverb with any value above 00 (manual). At that moment the backward memory is swelling into its last chord, Dmaj9, so the freeze holds it as a still picture after the sample cuts off.
  • The thaw, chain 80 on track 8, row 00: XRZ 00 on row 0. When the song loops back, the frozen chord melts into the intro, which starts on the same D: the song becomes a circle. Stop playback during the freeze to end on the still picture instead.

The freeze and the thaw. The manual says how to turn XRZ on and off, not whether sound arriving at a frozen reverb is added or ignored, or how cleanly the thaw lets go. If the thaw is rough, stop playback on the freeze instead. Report back: when the song loops, does the freeze let go cleanly at the downbeat?

The mix

The send effects are one per song; each instrument's MFX, DEL and REV decide how much reaches them. From the Mixer view, SHIFT+DOWN opens the effect settings. These are starting points by ear: the manual names the parameters but gives no scales.

  • SIZE E0, DECAY C0: a big, soft room, long enough that the lead's held notes leave a trace.
  • SHIMMER 30: a pitched-up feedback in the tail (6.2+). The lead sends the most (REV 70), so most of the shimmer is its tail. Keep it low enough that the chords don't sparkle.
  • MOD DEPTH 40, MOD FREQ 30: the tail drifts slowly, like the tape. INPUT EQ: cut the lows going in, so the bass and the chords' low notes stay out of the tail.
  • Delay TIME 0C: a dotted eighth (three rows), as in Low Tide, so the arp's and lead's echoes land between their notes. FEEDBACK 60: two or three repeats.
  • ModFX CHORUS: the tape's flutter, mostly on the chords (MFX 30).

That delay TIME 0C is a dotted eighth. The manual: "first digit is in beats, second digit is in fractions of a beat". If the second digit counts sixteenths of a beat, 0C is 3/4 of a beat; otherwise set it by ear. Report back: what does the help line say for 0C?

Two EQs

In an instrument, set EQ to a slot number, then EDIT on it (or SHIFT+RIGHT) opens the EQ editor. Set FREQ by watching the help line (whether the screen shows Hz or hex is Low Tide step 10's question).

  • EQ 04, on the lead: a low cut at about 350 Hz. The lead's lowest note is F♯5 (740 Hz); this only takes breath noise and rumble, and keeps them out of the reverb.
  • EQ 09, on the memory (09 and 0A): a low cut at about 150 Hz. At half speed the chords reach down to A2; the cut keeps the intro and breakdown clear of the live bass.

Balance and the limiter

  1. Leave the eight track volumes and the MX, DE and RE returns where they are, and balance with each instrument's DRY, so its sends stay put.
  2. DJF 80 (off), OTT 00.
  3. LIM: raise it from 00 until the limiter's line on the MIX meter moves only on the loudest rows (A2, A3 and A5). About 20 is a place to start.
