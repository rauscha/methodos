Struck bell

A small, pitched bell that rings and decays on its own, no envelope needed. It's the voice of Low Tide's hand-written arp (step 7): eighth notes through each chord's upper notes, an octave above the pad, with a dotted-eighth echo and a couple of notes left to chance. The phrase here is the arp's first bar.

The browser plays the notes on a plain tone; the bell's ring is the M8's.

Play it

One bar of Dm9 at 84 BPM. Rows 6 and C carry CHA 8F: the note plays about half the time, so the figure never repeats exactly. The other five bars of the arp are listed on step 7.

Why these values

  • SHAPE 20 STRUCK BELL: an additive bell model. TIMBRE sets the damping (how long it rings), COLOR the inharmonicity. COLOR 30 keeps it close to a pitched note, so it sits in a chord instead of clanging against it.
  • No volume envelope. A struck model makes its own decay; each note is a strike.
  • CUTOFF C0: just softens the highest partials.
  • DEL 50, REV 60: the echo is half the effect. In the effect settings (mixer, then delay), set TIME to a dotted eighth so the echoes land between the notes.
  • CHA 8F: in a phrase, X is the note's chance and Y the other FX's, from 0 (never) to F (always). 8 is about half.

Try

  • TIMBRE both ways: one end rings like a glass, the other is a dull clink.
  • COLOR 80 and up for a gamelan-like, out-of-tune bell. Lovely alone, sour in chords.
  • SHAPE 21 STRUCK DRUM for a metallic drum on the same figure.

Values are a starting point, not tried on an M8. Delay TIME's "first digit in beats, second in fractions of a beat" suggests 0C for a dotted eighth if the second digit counts sixteenths; what does the help text at the bottom of the screen say for 0C?
