Low Tide · step 10 of 10

The mix

The song is written. The mix makes room: each part gets its own space in pitch and in the stereo field, the reverb puts them in one room, and the limiter keeps it from clipping. All of it is done on the M8 by ear, and since 6.6 the EQ view shows a live spectrum, so you can also see what you're cutting.

This page is about the M8's own mixer; the demo above just plays the song for reference. Mix on headphones and check on the speaker.

1. Balance

  1. Start with the kick and bass alone: solo track 1 with OPTION+PLAY (release OPTION first to latch it), then track 2 the same way.
  2. Bring in the pad, then hats, arp and texture, one at a time. For each, ask whether you'd miss it if it stopped, and whether it covers anything already there.
  3. Turn parts down with the instrument's DRY first, so its reverb send stays where it is. The Mixer view's track volume moves the dry level and the sends together.
  4. OPTION+SHIFT+PLAY clears all mutes and solos.

2. EQ: a mono low end under a wide pad

The pad is wide (WIDTH C0) all the way down, so its low notes spread left and right on top of the mono bass, and they smear each other. The fix comes from Dirtywave's own meetup (#32, firmware 6.5.2), "particularly for Hypersynth": a low cut that only touches the side of the signal. The pad stays wide above, and goes mono below.

  1. In instrument 03, set EQ to a free slot number (03 is easy to remember). Then EDIT on it, or SHIFT+RIGHT, opens the EQ editor.
  2. On the LOW band: TYPE LOWCUT, MODE SIDE, FREQ about 500 Hz.
  3. Press PLAY and watch the spectrum. Bring FREQ down until the pad starts to sound narrow, then back up a little.
  4. Instrument 05 (the bell) can share it, or get its own slot with a plain STEREO low cut a little higher: the arp has nothing below its lowest F5 worth keeping.

MODE can be STEREO (both channels the same), MID (only what's common to both), SIDE (only the difference between them), LEFT or RIGHT. A double tap of EDIT in the EQ editor mutes and unmutes the EQ, which is the quickest before-and-after.

The EQ FREQ value for 500 Hz. The manual describes FREQ as the band's "center (or corner) frequency" but not how it's shown. Report back: does the screen show FREQ in Hz, or as hex?

3. One room: the sends

The reverb is shared, so the sends decide how far back each part sits. As set in the earlier steps:

PartREVWhy
Pad 0390Far back and huge; at the bottom of the tide, mostly reverb.
Bell 0560In the room, plus the delay (DEL 50).
Texture 0B40Distant.
Snare 0730A little space around it.
Hats 0818Barely.
Bass 0200Dry: reverb on the low end turns to mud.
Kick 0600Dry and in front.

The reverb itself is in the effect settings (Mixer view, then SHIFT+DOWN): a large SIZE and a long DECAY suit this song. Its INPUT EQ can cut the lows going into the reverb, which keeps the tail clear of the bass. Leave SHIMMER at 00 to start; a little later on is lovely on the pad.

4. The limiter

  1. In the Mixer view, LIM above 00 turns the main limiter on. Raise it until the white line on the MIX meter moves only on the loudest moments (the drops into rows 02 and 05).
  2. EDIT on LIM opens the Limiter and Mix Scope view. ATK near zero and REL 00 (auto) are good settings, and have been the defaults since firmware 5.0 (Dirtywave meetup). PEAK shows the loudest level so far.
  3. Any clipping shows as a bar on the meter; if you see it, lower MIX rather than pushing the limiter harder.
Listen forSwitch the pad's EQ off and on (double tap EDIT in the EQ editor): with it on, the bass gets firmer and the pad stays as wide. Then the whole song from row 00: the intro should feel deep, the full rows should be louder without being harsh, and the breakdown should feel like the room closing, not like the volume dropping.

When you're home

Project view, RENDER: MIXED writes one stereo file to /Renders, STEMS one per track. Set SONG ROW START 00 and LAST 06. Leave REVERB, DELAY and LIMITER on for a finished mix.
