The B section

Eight new bars, after your loop. Two things change at once: the chords slow down to two bars each, and one of them is borrowed from D minor. Over those bars a single line falls a half step at a time, B, B♭, A, and four tracks play it together. It's the moment the photo fades.

Listen forBar 13 of the roll (the B section's bar 5): Gmaj9 turns into Gm9 while the bass holds G and the top A holds. Nothing moves except two inner notes, B down to B♭ and F♯ down to F, and the chord darkens. The lead sags from B to B♭ at the same moment. Then bar 15 into 16 resolves inside the chord, D down to C♯, and your loop comes back.

  • The borrowed iv. Gmaj9 to Gm9 changes two notes, B to B♭ and F♯ to F, under the same bass note. Major IV to minor iv and home to I is one of the oldest nostalgic moves in pop.
  • Two shapes do new jobs. The major 7 shape (C4) played on G is Em9 without its root; on B♭ it's Gm9 without its root. The only new COLOR target is D4, for the sus.

The bass climbs

Your loop's bass falls (D, A, B, G); the B section's climbs: E1, the song's lowest note, then G1, G1, A1, and up a fourth to D2. Under both G chords it's the same bar (phrase 2C): the bass doesn't move while the chord darkens above it. Phrase 2E ends on B♭1, the fade line in the bass for one eighth.
