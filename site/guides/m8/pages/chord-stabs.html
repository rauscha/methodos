Chord stabs

A four-note chord in one sample, played on every root: short, round, a little worn, the tine of an FM electric piano softened by rendering. Overpass's stabs (step 4 makes the renders, step 5 plays them, step 7 adds the table). The trick is the deep-house one called chord memory: the shape never changes, only its root.

Each is the Glass keys FM patch on four tracks, one note each (third, seventh, ninth, fifth: rootless), rendered with QUICK RENDER, cropped to four beats, normalized, made mono and faded. Built on C, the note you type names the chord. The bass plays the roots.

The instrument

  • TBL. TIC 00: the table moves one row per stab (below).
  • DEGRADE 10, LOWPASS B0, RES 10: worn and round, the tine softened.
  • AHD on VOLUME, HOLD 02, DEC 10: 18 ticks, three-quarters of a beat: a hit and a little ring. 08 holds longer (HOLD 08, DEC 28, half a bar) for fewer, longer stabs.
  • AHD on CUTOFF, AMT 18, DEC 0C: a small brightening that closes in an eighth.
  • MFX 18: width for a mono sample. REV 38 (50 on 08).

A table that counts

At TIC 00 each stab moves the table on a row: firm, lazy (softer, darker, a few cents flat), reach (in between, a touch sharp), and back. Three against five stabs every two bars lines up only every six bars. Row 0 needs no commands: a note with an instrument number resets the instrument.
