Faded Polaroid · step 5 of 10

The drums

A soft, dusty beat from three factory samples, as if taped off the radio. Low Tide's drums were Macrosynth models; these are the Sampler. Kick and snare share track 5, the hats get track 6, and a light swing touches those two tracks only. The bass grows a small figure to go with the kick.

The demo plays no drums: a browser tone would only misrepresent a sample. Their phrases still light up as the song plays.

Three samples

  1. Instrument 05: TYPE SAMPLER. EDIT on LOAD, to the right of SAMPLE, opens the file browser; PLAY previews, EDIT loads. Find a soft, round kick with no click in the factory samples.
  2. Once one is loaded, EDIT+LEFT/RIGHT on its name steps through the other files in its folder: the quick way to audition.
  3. The same for 06 (a brushed rim, or a muffled clap) and 07 (a closed hat or a shaker). Any will do: the settings below dull them all.

  • TRANSP. OFF: the manual's advice for percussion. No transpose can ever move a drum.
  • DEGRADE is the grit; a little more on the hat, where it reads as dust. Any DEGRADE above 00 also turns off interpolation (manual).
  • LOWPASS on all three: dusty means the top is gone.
  • MOD 1 on each is an AHD on VOLUME, AMT FF, ATK 00. The envelopes, in ticks, keep each hit short whatever the sample's own tail: the kick 12 ticks (an eighth), the snare 15, the hat 6 (one row: closed).
  • Everything else stays at the Sampler's defaults: SLICE 00, PLAY FWD, START 00, LEN FF, DETUNE 80, RES 00, AMP 00, LIM CLIP, MFX and DEL 00.

A fill every fourth bar: NTH

NTH is a conditional trigger: it skips what's to its left (the note) or right (the other FX), depending on how many times the phrase has looped (manual). Written here as NTH 40, "the note plays on every fourth pass", so the same one-bar phrase plays the plain beat three times and the fill on the fourth. The manual leaves the counting to the help line.

How NTH counts. 40 is written for "the note plays every fourth pass". When you enter it, read the help line; if it says the note is skipped every fourth time, try NTH 04. Failing that, the fallback is phrase 51, the fill written out without NTH, with chain 05 as 50 50 50 51 50 50 50 51. Report back: what does the help line say for NTH 40?

The swing: groove 01, two tracks only

  1. From any phrase, SHIFT+UP opens the Groove view. OPTION+RIGHT moves to groove 01, so 00 stays straight for everything else.
  2. On row 0, EDIT+UP/DOWN changes rows 0 and 1 together, until they read 07 and 05. Rows 2 to F stay --, so the pair repeats.
  3. The GRV 01 on row 0 of phrases 50 and 60 puts tracks 5 and 6 on it. A GRV holds until another one.

7 + 5 = 12, the same as 6 + 6, so a bar still lasts a bar. The hats' two ghosts on rows 3 and B land a tick late; that's the lilt. Since 6.5 the groove view also shows the swing as a percentage. If 07/05 sounds too lilting, set the groove to 48 PPQN and use 0D 0B, about 54%.

The bass grows a figure

With the kick on the "and" of 3, the bass gets a quiet octave there, and a soft step into the next root on the last eighth. The next root scoops in from it with PSL 02. Chain 21 replaces chain 02 on track 2.

Row 0 is V 70. Staff, bar 1: D2 for a half and an eighth, D3 a quarter, B1 an eighth. The B1 and the E2 in bar 4 are passing notes, the sixth of their chord, there for one quiet eighth.

PSL glides into its note from the one before over a number of ticks; 02 is a quick scoop. Since 6.6.3 a note with an instrument number resets PSL (changelog), so it doesn't leak into the rest of the bar.

Listen forThe beat should feel more than hear itself: a pillow of a kick, a snare like a brush, the hats barely there. Every fourth bar three whispers rise into the next downbeat. The arp doesn't swing; only the hats' ghosts do.
