The pad sound

Before writing any chords, make the sound on one chord and hold it. Shaping a pad is about time: how slowly it arrives, how long it hangs after you let go, and how it changes while it's held. That's easier to judge on one chord than on eight bars of them.

One note in phrase 30

  1. In the Song view, put the cursor on track 3, row 00, and press EDIT to make a chain. Set it to 03 with EDIT+arrows.
  2. SHIFT+RIGHT into the chain. On row 0, make phrase 30.
  3. SHIFT+RIGHT into the phrase. On row 0 enter the note D-3 and set the I column to 03.

Nothing else in the phrase. With no OFF, the note keeps sounding, and because the chain is one phrase long, it plays again every bar. That D-3 is the only note the pad will ever play. The chord comes from the instrument.

Instrument 03: Hypersynth

SHIFT+RIGHT from the phrase into instrument 03 and set TYPE to HYPERSYNTH. It's a chord synth: up to six notes per chord, each one a pair of detuned saws, from 16 chord banks you program yourself.

Bank 00

You found on 6.6.3 that the CHORD row reads CHORD 00 ▸ 00 04 07 0B 00 00: the bank number, then six interval slots, with a keyboard under it showing the notes. Leave the bank at 00 and set its slots to 03 0A 0E 13 18. Those are semitones above the played note, in hex: on D-3 that's F3 C4 E4 A4 D5, Dm9 with no D at the bottom. The keyboard under the row should show those five notes.

Intervals above an octave. 13 and 18 are 19 and 24 semitones, past the one-octave mark at 0C. The changelog says chord values "can now jump an octave" with EDIT+UP/DOWN, which suggests they go that far. Report back: does 18 go in, and does the keyboard show D5?

Remove slot 6. Bank 00 uses five slots, and a slot left at 00 plays the held D itself, stacked on the chord. You found that OPTION+EDIT (the delete shortcut) on a slot of the CHORD row removes it. Do that on the sixth slot of every bank you use.

The best SWARM. 08 is a starting point; the curve between 00 and 80 isn't published. Try 04 to 10 on the held chord. (Not 00: at 00 the saws freeze in phase.) Report back: the SWARM value you like.

Make it breathe

SHIFT+UP from the instrument opens its four modulation slots.

MOD 1: a slow swell

An ADSR on VOLUME. You found on 6.6.3 that ADSR times are in seconds, not ticks: ATK 60 reads "0.200 SECONDS" in the help line at the bottom of the screen. Tim said the same in the firmware 4.0 meetup: AHD counts ticks, ADSR counts time. So the hex values don't map to bars, and these pages give the time to aim for instead.

  • ATK C2: the help line reads 2.869 s. That's one bar at 84 BPM (four beats of 0.714 s), so the pad takes a full bar to arrive. You found the value.
  • REL CB: 3.663 s, about a bar and a quarter, your choice. After an OFF the pad fades over that long. For a full two bars, raise it until the help line reads about 5.71 s.
  • DEC 00, SUS FF: no decay; it holds at full level.

How the hex maps to seconds isn't published (the changelog only says the low range was rescaled). Three points you've read off the screen: 60 = 0.200 s, C2 = 2.869 s, CB = 3.663 s. Set any other ADSR by the help line.

Because ADSR is in seconds, these times are right only at 84 BPM. At another tempo, work out the bar length (240 divided by the BPM) and set them again by the help line.

An ADSR only releases on an OFF (a note OFF or the OFF command). This phrase has none, so while it plays, the pad just holds at the top. Press Stop on the strip to send an OFF and watch the release.The drawing below marks where an OFF would start the release.

The phrase retriggers the note every bar. Since 6.6, a retrigger of the same instrument resumes the attack from wherever the envelope already is, so a held pad doesn't dip each bar. In step 3 the note is struck only once every eight bars, and the chords change without striking it at all.

MOD 2: the tide

An LFO on CUTOFF: OSC SIN for a smooth sweep, TRIG FREE so it keeps running across notes instead of restarting on each one, AMT 40 for a modest sweep, and FREQ 80: one slow cycle every eight bars.

LFO FREQ is a length, not a speed: you found that FREQ 80 reads "8 PHRASES 0 STEPS 0 TICKS". Higher is slower. 10 is one phrase (16 steps, one bar here), 80 is eight. Unlike the ADSR, it counts steps, so the tide stays eight bars long at any tempo.

How wide the sweep drawn above is (about an octave either way) is the demo'sdrawing's own reading of AMT 40, not an M8 fact. Trust your ears on the device.

Listen forPress PLAY in the phrase view and wait. The chord takes a full bar to arrive. Then, over the next eight bars, the tone darkens until it's almost muffled and slowly opens up again. That's the tide. When it's at its darkest, the reverb is most of what you hear. The saws should shimmer slowly, not buzz.

Things to try

  • SWARM 20: more motion, and the start of the buzz. Hear where it tips over, then go back down.
  • LFO OSC TRI: the sweep turns at the ends instead of lingering.
  • ATK at about 1.43 s: half a bar. Quicker, less dreamlike.
  • Press PLAY in the instrument view, then EDIT+LEFT/RIGHT on CUTOFF, to hear by hand what the LFO is doing for you.
