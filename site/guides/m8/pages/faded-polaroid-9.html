Faded Polaroid · step 9 of 10

The memory

The song opens and closes with itself: your A section rendered to a sample on the M8, played back an octave down at half speed, dulled, and in the outro played backward. This step makes that sample. It's resampling: turning part of your song into a sample you can play like any other. The finished instrument is the memory card; Sampling has the background.

The demo can't play your render, so track 7 is silent here. Its chain and phrase still light up on row 02.

Pitch is speed

A Sampler plays a sample slower to play it lower. One octave down is exactly half speed, so four bars last eight. Render your four bars, play them an octave below their own pitch, and they fill one eight-bar song row: the same chords, slower and lower, and duller, because slowing a sample also brings its top end down. That dullness is the point.

Render your A section, without the bass

Half speed would put the bass at D1 to G0, below hearing and full of mud. So the render leaves it out. Rather than mute a track and wonder whether the render honors mutes, it's made from a spare song row holding only the arp and the chords.

  1. A spare row. Song view, row 0F (any empty row will do): track 1 chain 01, track 3 chain 03. Leave track 2 empty.
  2. Select it. Cursor on row 0F, track 1. SHIFT+OPTION starts a selection; RIGHT twice covers tracks 1 to 3 of that row.
  3. Render. Double-tap EDIT. The dialog offers QUICK RENDER, REALTIME and EXTERNAL ONLY; choose QUICK RENDER (offline, the tracks only). OPTION would cancel.
  4. Find it. When it finishes, a new Sampler instrument holding the render appears and becomes the default for new notes. Note its number.
  5. Open the sample editor. In that instrument, EDIT on EDIT, to the right of SAMPLE.
  6. Select four bars. On SELECT, leave the start at the beginning. Move the end with OPTION+UP/DOWN, which steps in beats at the song's tempo, to 16 beats: the end of your bar 4. The help line shows the position. (The render is eight bars, your loop twice.)
  7. Crop. On PROCESS, EDIT+LEFT/RIGHT to CROP, then EDIT.
  8. Soften the cut. SELECT the last half beat (from 15 beats 2 steps to 16 beats; OPTION+LEFT/RIGHT steps by a sixteenth). PROCESS: the fade-out (FADE IN/OUT). Bar 4's Gmaj9 is still at full level where the crop cuts it; the fade keeps the end of the intro from clicking, and the start of the backward copy from clicking in.
  9. Name and save. NAME FADED, then SAVE. OPTION leaves the editor.
  10. Move it to 09. In the new instrument, SHIFT+OPTION copies it. OPTION+LEFT/RIGHT to instrument 09; SHIFT+EDIT pastes. Then clear the slot the render landed in: in the Instrument Pool view, EDIT+OPTION on its name. (Or keep the number it landed on and type that wherever these pages say 09.)
  11. Set 09 as below.
  12. Make 0A. On 09, SHIFT+OPTION; OPTION+RIGHT to 0A; SHIFT+EDIT. Then PLAY REV, REV 60, NAME FADED REV.
  13. Clear row 0F: EDIT+OPTION on each of its cells.
  14. Check the length. Play row 02 (below): the memory should end exactly as the row does. 6.6 fixed a quick-render length error; if it runs long or short, trim LEN.

Whether QUICK RENDER includes the send effects (reverb, delay, chorus). Either way works: with them, the haze is baked in; without, 09's own sends add it. Report back: which was it?

Instruments 09 and 0A

  • PLAY FWD, LOOP ST. 00: once, forward, not looping. One note plays the whole song row and ends with it.
  • DEGRADE 30, CUTOFF 70: grainy, and duller than anything live, so it sits behind.
  • DRY 90, MFX 20, DEL 00, REV 50: quiet and far back; a little chorus for more tape warble; no echoes, which would blur the slowed chords.
  • EQ 09: a low cut, set up in step 10. At half speed the chords reach down to A2.
  • MOD 1, LFO on PITCH: SIN, FREE, AMT 02, FREQ 20: a deeper, faster wow than the live parts, one cycle every two bars. The memory is the more worn copy.
  • No volume envelope: the sample plays to its own end, which the crop put at the end of the row.

0A is the same with PLAY REV and REV 60: once, backward, so every pluck and chord swells in and cuts off. It sends more reverb so the outro's freeze (step 10) has a chord to hold.

Where PLAY REV starts. Written as "from the end, backward". If 0A plays nothing, or only part, use a reversed file instead: in 0A's sample editor, SELECT all, PROCESS REVERSE, NAME FADEDREV, SAVE (a new file, so 09 still plays forward), and set 0A to PLAY FWD. Report back: which did you need?

Play it on C-3

  1. Song view, row 02, track 7: chain 07. In it, phrase 70 on row 0 and the empty phrase FE on rows 1 to 7.
  2. Phrase 70, row 0: C-3, V 60, I 09. One note, eight bars.
  3. On tracks 1 to 6 of row 02, and track 7 of rows 00 and 01, put chain FE, the silence chain, as in Low Tide's step 9: phrase FD (an OFF on row 0) on row 0, the empty phrase FE on rows 1 to 7. An empty cell would send that track back to the start of its list (manual), out of step with the others. Then the song plays your loop, the B section, and the memory on its own.

That C-4 plays a sample at its own pitch, so C-3 is half speed. The Sampling page asks the same; the manual's pitch limit starts above C-4. Report back: does the memory on C-3 sound exactly an octave below your loop?

Listen forYour four bars, now eight, an octave down: the arp's eighths have become slow quarters, the chords last two bars each, and everything is softer at the top and gently warbling. It should sound like your song heard through a wall, a long time ago. Then try 0A on C-3: the same, backward, every note swelling in.

Things to try

  • C-2 instead of C-3: two octaves down, quarter speed, sixteen bars. Too long for one row, and very dull, but worth hearing.
  • DEGRADE 00 for a cleaner memory; 60 for a cheaper one.
