How these pages work

Every page in this guide is something to read with the M8 in your hands. Some pages are mostly text. The ones that build a song also have a strip across the top that plays the song so far, and pictures that move with it.

The strip at the top

Play starts the song from the top and loops it. Stop sends every note an OFF, so you can watch and hear the releases ring out. Each track you can hear has a button that mutes it; a muted track is struck through and drawn with a dashed outline. The strip stays at the top while you scroll.

The sound is a stand-in made in the browser, not the M8: the notes, not the tone. It plays the same notes, envelopes and filter moves the page tells you to enter, so it's right about what happens and when. Drums and samples make no sound in it (a browser tone would only misrepresent them), but their phrases still draw, and the kick still makes the pad duck.

The pictures

  • Copies of the M8's screens (song, phrase, chain, table, instrument) show exactly what to type. A row or value marked NEW, with a bar in the left margin, is what this step adds. While the song plays, a ▶ cursor and bold type mark the row the M8 would be on. A row with no note shows --- in the note columns and only its command.
  • The piano roll shows every track as notes on a keyboard, with the chord names along the top. The pad's notes are outlined and hatched, everything else is solid, and the pad's top voice is drawn as a thick line, so you can follow the melody hidden in the chords. When a held chord changes without being struck again, the roll starts a new block.
  • The progression shows each chord with its Roman numeral. The chord sounding now is filled dark and marked "now"; the next one has a thick outline and says "next". Each card says how the harmony moves on.
  • Envelopes draw the shape, with the note's length underneath and a mark where the OFF arrives. A dot rides the shape as the song plays. The duck (step 6) is drawn over one bar, with a solid triangle under each kick.
  • Filters show the cutoff moving over time, and the filter's curve laid over what the track is actually playing.

Units

The M8 counts time three ways, and these pages say which each time it matters:

  • ADSR envelopes: seconds. You found on 6.6.3 that ATK 60 reads "0.200 SECONDS". The pages give a target ("until the help line reads 2.86 s") rather than a hex value.
  • AHD and trig envelopes: ticks, 6 to a row, 24 to a beat. AHD ATK 10 reads "2 STEPS 4 TICKS".
  • LFO FREQ: a length in steps. FREQ 80 reads "8 PHRASES 0 STEPS 0 TICKS". Higher is slower; 10 is one phrase.

Things marked unverified

A dashed box like this one means something Claude couldn't confirm in the manual for 6.5.2, the changelog up to 6.6.3, or Dirtywave's meetups: a button press, a parameter's units, how a value is written. Each box ends with one thing to check on the M8.

Report back

The five that matter most. A text with the answers, and a photo or two, fixes the pages; the other dashed boxes can wait.

  1. The pad's ADSR: the ATK and REL values whose help line reads 2.86 s and 5.71 s. (step 2)
  2. The SWARM value you like on the held chord, somewhere around 04 to 10. (step 2)
  3. The duck: the trig envelope AMT whose help line reads about minus half. (step 6)
  4. The delay: what the help line says for TIME 0C. (step 7)
  5. After pressing Play on any step: did any picture tell you something the words didn't? Which one?
