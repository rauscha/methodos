The drums

One drum file, cut into slices, and the slices re-sequenced as a 2-step beat: a broken kick, a backbeat clap, swung hats. Then a heavier swing than Low Tide's, on every track at once, and a clap roll that builds into a drop. New here: slicing, and RET.

Slice one file

  1. Load it. Instrument 01: TYPE SAMPLER. EDIT on LOAD opens the file browser; PLAY previews, EDIT loads. Ideally one file with a short kick, a dry clap, a rim, a closed hat, an open hat and a shaker; a break works too.
  2. Open the sample editor: EDIT on EDIT.
  3. Mark the hits. Either PROCESS SLICE:AUTO, which finds the hits, or chop by ear: cursor on SLICE MARKER, press PLAY, and tap EDIT on each hit ("lazy chop", since 5.0).
  4. Save it as your own file. NAME OVERKIT, SAVE. A new name keeps the factory file as it was; the markers are stored in the WAV. OPTION leaves.
  5. Play the markers: SLICE 01 FILE. The first slice plays on C-1, the next on C#1, and on up (manual).
  6. Find the six sounds. Play C-1 upward and write down which note is which.

A lazy chop of a break puts the slices in the order the break plays them, which won't be this one. Then change only the N column of the drum phrases: the rhythms stay.

SLICE's values. The manual names 01 FILE for markers but not what the other values read (the Sampling page asks too). If 01 FILE doesn't play your markers, step SLICE through its values and watch the help line. Report back: what does SLICE read for its first few values?

  • TRANSP. OFF on all three: a transpose would play the wrong slices.
  • DEGRADE 00: crisp. The worn sound belongs to the stabs and the voice.
  • The kick keeps its low end, with a little more push (AMP 10); the clap and hats lose theirs through a high-pass.
  • MOD 1 on each is an AHD on VOLUME, AMT FF, ATK 00, in ticks: the kick 9 ticks, tight, so the next slice never bleeds in; the clap 16; the hats an eighth, long enough for the open hat to ring over the row after it. The next hat or shaker cuts the closed ones.

Swing everything: groove 00

Low Tide put a second groove on two tracks. Here every track swings the same, so edit groove 00 itself, the default every track plays (manual: "Groove 00 is the default groove for all 8 tracks"). No phrase needs a GRV.

  1. From any phrase, SHIFT+UP opens the groove view, on groove 00.
  2. On row 0, EDIT+UP/DOWN changes rows 0 and 1 together until they read 08 and 04. Rows 2 to F stay --.

08/04 is about 67% (the groove view shows the percentage since 6.5): every odd row lands two-thirds of the way through its eighth. That's the shaker, the kick's ghost, the rim, the stab on "1a", and later the voice's stutter. If it sounds like a shuffle rather than garage, try 48 PPQN and 0F 09, about 62%.

The build: RET and KIL

Bars 7 and 8 of chain 23 build into the top of the loop. In the finished song they lead into the drop after the breakdown.

  • Phrase 22: eighth-note claps, velocity rising from 14 to 30.
  • Phrase 23, row 0: RET 96. RET retriggers the row: Y is the ticks between retrigs, and X from 8 to F makes each one louder (0 to 7, quieter). So 96 is a clap every 6 ticks, sixteenths, rising.
  • Row 8: RET A3: every 3 ticks, thirty-seconds, louder faster.
  • Row E: KIL 00: stops the clap at once. An eighth of silence before the drop.

How far a RET reaches. It's written as if it keeps retriggering until the next note or a KIL; the 6.6.3 changelog fixed "a note or OFF command could possibly not stop an active RET", which points that way. If it only fills its own row, write the clap on every row of phrase 23 with the same RETs. Report back: does the build roll right through bar 8?

Listen forThe missing kick on beat 3: the beat seems to trip and catch itself every bar. The shaker is felt more than heard, swung hard; mute track 3 and the groove goes stiff. Bar 8: the roll tightens and rises, then an eighth of nothing.

Things to try

  • The spare second kick (C#1) on row F of phrase 11 instead of the ghost.
  • RET 46 on phrase 23's row 0: an X below 8 makes each retrig quieter, so the roll falls away instead of rising.
