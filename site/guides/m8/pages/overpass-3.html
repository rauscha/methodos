Four tracks, one chord

An M8 track plays one note at a time, and the FM synth has no chord mode. So a chord takes four tracks: the same instrument on tracks 5 to 8, one note each, all on the same row. Two chords, one bar each, with a bar of silence after each for the ring. Next step they become samples.

Listen forRow 00: four tines at once, then one warm minor chord ringing out over the bar. Row 01: the same, but brighter. Only the bottom note moved, E♭ up to E. That one half step is the whole difference between the song's cold half and its warm half.

  • Rootless, as in the other two songs: the third, the seventh, the ninth and the fifth, upward. No C. The bass will play every root.
  • Built on C because the render will be played on other notes, and a Sampler moves a sample by the distance from C-4 (if C-4 is its own pitch: step 5 asks). Built on C, the note you type names the chord: A-3 plays Am9, D-4 plays Dm9.
  • An open voicing, a fourth or more between most neighbors, so the chord stays clear when it's played low.

Enter it

  1. Song view, row 00: chains 90, 91, 92, 93 on tracks 5, 6, 7 and 8. Row 01: 94 to 97.
  2. Each chain is two rows: the chord's phrase, then FE, an empty phrase, so the note rings through a second bar. That bar is the tail the render needs.
  3. Each phrase is one note on row 0: V 60, I 0A. The M8 spells E♭ and B♭ as D# and A#.

That one FM instrument on four tracks gives four independent voices. It should: each track plays its own note. If row 00 sounds like one note, or notes cut each other off, copy 0A to three more slots and give each track its own copy. Report back: does row 00 sound four notes?

Things to try

  • Mute tracks 5 to 7 (OPTION+SHIFT on each) and listen to the top voice alone, then add the others back one at a time.
  • Move track 8's G5 down an octave to G4: a closed voicing, muddier. Back to G5.
