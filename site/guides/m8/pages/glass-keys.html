Glass keys

An electric piano with a glassy tine on the front of each note: a bright "tink" that fades in an eighth note, over a round body that rings for a bar. The Glass Garden arp gets a second chance; in the Faded Polaroid session its FM voice "wasn't becoming very crystalline".

The browser plays the notes on a plain tone; FM is the M8's.

The operators

ALGO 07 is [A>B]+[C>D]: two pairs, side by side. In each pair the first operator bends the second, and you hear the second. So B and D are what you hear; A and C only color them.

Play it

Bar 1 of the Glass Garden arp, Dmaj9, at its 92 BPM.

Why these values

  • C at ratio 14.00 into D at 1.00: a modulator fourteen times the note's frequency puts a cluster of high, slightly bell-like partials on D. That's the glass.
  • C's MOD slot set to 1 LEV, and MOD 2 (AHD, DEC 0C) on MOD1: the envelope pulls C's level from full to nothing in an eighth note. The sparkle is only on the attack, as on a real tine.
  • MOD 1, AHD on VOLUME, DEC 60: the whole note fades over a bar.
  • A at 1.00 into B, low level (30): a gentle body. More A level makes it reedier.

Why Glass Garden may have missed. The chat's patch set A to level 80 and B to 24, at ratios 1.00 and 3.00. If B is the one you hear, as the algorithm's name suggests, its 3.00 ratio plays an octave and a fifth above the note, quietly, under a loud modulator. Worth a look.

Try

  • C's ratio at 7.00 or 3.50 for a different glass.
  • MOD 2's DEC at 18 (a beat) for a longer shimmer.
  • A little FB on A for a dirtier, harder body.

Values are a starting point, not tried on an M8.
