Chord memory

One sample, one chord shape, played on every root: the whole progression from the render you just made. Every voice moves the same distance, so the chord slides around as one block. That's the deep-house sound called chord memory, and the reason the renders were built on C.

Listen forThe stabs never land on a kick's beat: they fall between, on the "a" of 1 and the "and"s. In bar 8 the chord rises a step and its top note brightens, F♯ instead of F: the loop leans home. In the B section (row 01) the stabs are fewer, ring longer, and the chord is warmer: the major render.

  • A-3 is three semitones below C-4, so the Cm9 render plays three semitones down: E♭ B♭ D G becomes C G B E, Am9 without its root.
  • The shape doesn't know the key. Dm9 brings F natural; Em9 brings F♯, the one bright note, from A Dorian. Same idea as Low Tide's C♯: one note from outside the key at the turnaround.
  • The top voice is always the chord's fifth: E, A, B. Em9 sits at E-4, not E-3, so its F♯ is F♯5, the note your voice will join at the peak.
  • The renders are played from seven semitones below their own pitch (F-3) to four above (E-4).
