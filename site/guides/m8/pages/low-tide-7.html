The arp

A small bell plays eighth notes through each chord's upper notes, an octave above the pad, with a dotted-eighth echo and a few notes left to chance. It's written by hand, one phrase per chord, because the ARP command can't follow chords: it plays the note plus two fixed intervals. The voice is the Struck bell card, instrument 05.

The arp's notes sit above the pad's top line and keep touching it: D over D in bar 1, E against the pad's E in bar 2. In bar 8 it leans on C♯ three times, and the loop's first note is the D it was waiting for.

  1. Song view, track 4, row 00: chain 04. In it: 40, 41, 42, 42, 43, 43, 44, 45. The B♭ and G bars repeat their phrase; the chance notes keep the repeat from sounding like one.
  2. Enter phrase 40. For the next ones, clone: on the phrase number in the chain, SHIFT+OPTION then EDIT copies it to a new number. Then change the notes: EDIT+LEFT/RIGHT moves a note by a semitone, EDIT+UP/DOWN by an octave.
  3. Instrument 05 as on the card: MACROSYN, SHAPE 20 STRUCK BELL, TIMBRE 50, COLOR 30, CUTOFF C0, DRY A0, DEL 50, REV 60. No envelope: a struck model makes its own decay.

Why these notes

  • Each bar uses the chord's color notes (9ths, 7ths, 5ths) more than its root. Over B♭ the arp never plays B♭; over G it never plays G. The bass has the root.
  • The line moves mostly by leaps of a fourth or fifth, which a bell rings through well, and its highest notes land on the beat, where the velocity is 60.
  • Bar 8 is built around C♯, the leading tone, so the D on bar 1 row 0 is a resolution. The roll shows the arp's C♯6 above the pad's C♯5: the same pull in two octaves.

Chance: CHA

CHA XY in a phrase sets two odds, from 0 (never) to F (always): X for the note, Y for the other FX on the row. CHA 8F gives the note about an even chance and leaves the FX alone. A note or two per bar drops out at random, so eight bars of the same figure never repeat exactly.

The echo: delay

DEL 50 on the instrument sends it to the delay. Set the delay in the effect settings: from the Mixer view, SHIFT+DOWN.

  • TIME to a dotted eighth (three rows), so each echo lands between two notes.
  • FEEDBACK low to middling: two or three audible repeats.
  • REVERB SEND up a little, so the echoes sit in the same room as the pad.

The delay TIME value for a dotted eighth. The manual: "First digit is in beats, second digit is in fractions of a beat. Ex: 40 is 4 beats long". If the second digit counts sixteenths of a beat, a dotted eighth (3/4 of a beat) is 0C. Report back: what does the help line say for 0C?

Solo track 4 (OPTION+PLAY): the bell and its echo should interlock into a steady sixteenth-note shimmer, with gaps where CHA drops a note. Then unsolo: it should sit on top of the pad, not inside it.

Things to try

  • TIMBRE lower for a duller clink, higher for a long glassy ring (it's the damping).
  • CHA 4F on more rows: sparser, more like rain.
  • The arp written as a table instead: see Tables, which shows why a table can follow the root but not the chord.
