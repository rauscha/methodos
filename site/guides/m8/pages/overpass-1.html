Overpass · step 1 of 10

The plan and the setup

Overpass is a night song built from samples, most of which you make yourself on the M8. 124 BPM in A minor: 2-step garage meeting deep house. A broken kick, a backbeat clap, swung hats, warm chord stabs, and your own voice in fragments. It's the guide's FM song, its sampling song, and its second table lesson, the opposite of Faded Polaroid's.

The mood

After the last train, walking under an overpass with the rain stopped. Cold concrete and wet glass outside, warmth in the chords, a voice that only ever says half a phrase, a beat that keeps skipping a step. Lonely but not bleak: the warm keys are the lit windows. For the feel: Burial's "Archangel" (vocal fragments as the hook, the 2-step skip), Mr. Fingers' "Can You Feel It" (unhurried deep house, warm minor chords that sit and glow), Todd Edwards (melodies made of chopped vocal snippets).

Draft choice.This is a turn: faster and beat-led after two slow songs. These pages write it as the plan has it. If you'd rather a third slow song, say so before you start; nothing in the other songs depends on it.

The idea: the M8 can't play an FM chord

The FM synth plays one note per track. So the chords are made the long way round: four tracks each play one note of a chord on the same FM electric piano, and the M8 renders those four tracks to a sample. A Sampler then plays that one sample on every root. That's the deep-house trick called chord memory: one chord shape, moved around whole.

  • Two renders, each a rootless four-note chord built on C: a minor ninth and a major ninth. Because they're built on C, the note you type names the chord: A-3 plays Am9.
  • The A section moves one minor shape in parallel: Am9, Dm9, Am9, then Dm9 and Em9. Em9 brings F♯, the one bright note, from A Dorian, in the last bar: it lifts the loop home.
  • The B section switches to the major shape on F and G, ♭VI and ♭VII, climbing in whole steps out of the tunnel.
  • The FM bass plays every root, arriving at each new chord an eighth early, sliding in.

Set up the song

  1. Save whatever you're working on. Project view: NEW.
  2. TEMPO 124. NAME OVERPASS, SAVE.
  3. Leave SCALE alone: the Em9 bar's F♯ isn't in A minor, and a scale would bend it.
  4. Leave GROOVE on 00. In step 6 you edit groove 00 itself, so every track swings the same.

Before you start

  • One drum file to slice from the factory samples, for step 6: ideally one file with a short kick, a dry clap, a rim, a closed hat, an open hat and a shaker. A drum break works too; a snare can stand in for the clap.
  • The rain, for step 10: Low Tide's FIELD sample if it was rain or traffic, or a new recording on the mic.
  • Headphones for step 9, so the reference tone doesn't leak into the mic.
  • Free slots: instruments 01 to 0C (and 0D for a moment), tables 05 and 08, and a spare song row, 0F.
