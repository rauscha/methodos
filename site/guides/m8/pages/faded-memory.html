The memory

Your own four bars, rendered on the M8 and played back an octave down at half speed: the same chords, slower, lower, duller and gently warbling, as if remembered. Played backward, every note swells in and cuts off. Faded Polaroid's intro, breakdown and outro (step 9 has the render step by step). The idea works on any loop.

Make it

  1. A spare song row with only the parts you want (no bass: half speed puts it below hearing).
  2. Select those cells (SHIFT+OPTION, then arrows), double-tap EDIT, choose QUICK RENDER.
  3. In the new instrument's sample editor: SELECT exactly the bars you want (OPTION+UP/DOWN steps in beats), CROP, a short fade-out at the end, NAME, SAVE.
  4. Set it as below, then copy it to a second slot with PLAY REV for the backward version.

The instrument

  • Played on C-3: an octave below its own pitch, so half speed. Four bars fill eight.
  • PLAY FWD, not looping: one note plays the whole row and ends with it; the crop sets the length.
  • DEGRADE 30, LOWPASS 70: grainy and dull, so it sits behind anything live. Slowing it down already took its top end down.
  • LFO on PITCH, AMT 02, FREQ 20: one wow every two bars, deeper than the live parts' tape wobble.
  • DRY 90, REV 50 (60 on the backward copy, 0A), no delay: echoes would blur the slowed chords.

Play it

Try

  • C-2: two octaves down, quarter speed. Very dull, very long.
  • The backward copy under a freeze (XRZ) on its last chord, as Faded Polaroid ends.
