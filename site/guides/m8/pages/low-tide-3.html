Low Tide · step 3 of 10

The chords

Now the pad gets its eight bars, and a second track holds the roots underneath. The pad keeps playing the one D-3 from step 2. Each chord is a bank of intervals above it, and a command on an empty row switches banks while the note keeps sounding.

Press Play and watch the NOW mark move down the list. The pad never re-attacks: each chord melts into the next. In the roll, bar 1 to bar 3 moves one note, E up to F, and the chord changes anyway because the root under it falls from D to B♭.

Five more chord banks

Back in instrument 03, on the CHORD row: move the cursor onto the bank number, set it to 01 with EDIT+arrows, then fill that bank's slots. Repeat for banks 02 to 05. Bank 00 is already Dm9 from step 2. Every interval counts up from the D-3 in the phrase.

The voicings are open on purpose, after your note from the plane that tight chords turned the swarming saws into a buzz. No two neighboring notes are closer than a minor third, and most are a fourth or more apart. Voice leading stays smooth anyway: from one bank to the next, most voices hold and the rest move by a step.

  • Banks 00 and 01 are both Dm9; 01 lifts the top voice to E for bar 2.
  • Banks 04 and 05 drop to E3 at the bottom and keep five voices, so the chord doesn't thin out on the dominant. Bank 05's C♯ is the loop's one bright note.
  • Every bank uses five slots. Remove the sixth with OPTION+EDIT, as in step 2, or it plays the held D.

Changing banks with CRD

You found the command on 6.6.3: CRD. Its value picks the bank, and on a row with no note it leaves the held note sounding: no re-attack. That's the whole trick. Phrase 30 strikes D-3 once; every later bar changes the chord with CRD alone, and the ADSR never starts again.

  1. In phrase 30, on row 0, put the cursor on FX1, press EDIT+UP to open the FX help list, pick CRD, and set it to 00. That puts the loop back on bank 00 each time it comes round.
  2. In the chain, make phrases 31 to 35. EDIT on an empty row inserts a phrase number; EDIT+UP/RIGHT dials it. (EDIT twice makes a new phrase at the lowest free number instead; that works too, if you don't mind different numbers.)
  3. In each of them, row 0 has no note and no instrument, just CRD in FX1 with the bank number.

The pad's chain

Phrase 3F, an empty phrase, fills bars 4 and 6, where the chord holds. An empty phrase has no notes and no OFF, so whatever was sounding keeps sounding. An empty row in the chain would be different: it ends the chain.

The roots: track 2

A low floor that holds each root and hides inside the pad: you should feel the chord change more than hear a bass. Step 4 builds the bassline on this same instrument.

  1. In the Song view, track 2, row 00: chain 02.
  2. In the chain: phrases 20, 2F, 21, 2F, 22, 2F, 23, 2F. Phrase 2F stays empty, like 3F.
  3. In phrases 20 to 23, one note on row 0, instrument 02.
  4. Instrument 02: TYPE MACROSYN, SHAPE SINE TRIANGLE. A sine and a triangle into a wavefolder; COLOR balances the two, TIMBRE folds them. Set them as below.
  • TIMBRE 00, COLOR 10: no folding and almost pure sine, so it has few harmonics to poke out above the pad.
  • CUTOFF 50, RES 00, AMP 00: rounds off what's left.
  • DRY 80, REV 00: quiet, and dry. Reverb on the low end turns to mud; step 10 has the EQ trick that keeps the pad's own low end out of its way.

If it's still too present, lower DRY first. An EQ low shelf (step 10) is the second knob.

Two mods so it breathes with the pad

  • MOD 1, ADSR on VOLUME: ATK until the help line reads about 0.5 s, SUS FF, REL about 1.0 s. The soft attack is heard when a root comes in from silence (the very start, and in step 9 when the bass enters after a rest). Between roots there's no OFF, so since 6.6 the next note resumes from full level and the floor doesn't dip.
  • MOD 2, LFO on CUTOFF: OSC SIN, TRIG FREE, AMT 20, FREQ 80. The same eight-bar tide as the pad, smaller, so the roots darken and open with it and read as part of the pad.

Whether the two tides line up. Both LFOs are FREE, so each keeps its own phase, and the manual doesn't say where a FREE LFO starts. Report back: playing from the top, do the pad and the roots darken together?

The M8 spells every flat as a sharp, so B♭1 is entered as A#1.

Mute T2 on the strip and listen to bars 1 and 3: nearly the same pad notes, which sound like nearly the same chord. Unmute it and they're different chords. Then listen to the end of bar 8 into bar 1, where four pad voices step up at once and the C♯ lands on D.

If it still buzzes

  • Lower SWARM first (step 2 starts it at 08). That's the detune, and the main cause.
  • Then lower WIDTH a little, or close CUTOFF: buzz lives in the upper harmonics.
  • DARK SAW or TRIANGLE as the SHAPE takes the edge off every voice at once.
