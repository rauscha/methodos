Hummed voice

One hummed "ooh" on C, recorded on the M8's mic, played as a melody: breathy, close, a little lo-fi, and chopped, every note starting at the front of the hum and cut hard by the next. A backward copy swells in; a dub copy throws single notes into the delay. Overpass's hook (step 9 has the recording step by step).

Record it

  1. A reference tone on C (a Wavsynth SINE holding C-5 in a spare row) and headphones on.
  2. On a free instrument: TYPE SAMPLER, EDIT on REC., SRC MIC, VOL set by humming.
  3. Start with the power button; hum one "ooh" on the C in head voice for 3 or 4 seconds, ending softly.
  4. SELECT from the start of the hum to about 1.8 s; CROP; MONO: MIX; NORMALIZE; a short fade at the end; NAME VOICE C; SAVE.
  5. Tune DETUNE against the reference until C-4 and the tone sound as one.

The instrument

  • PLAY FWD, not looping: each note from the front of the hum.
  • ADSR on VOLUME, ATK about 0.005 s, REL about 0.030 s by the help line: a tiny fade each way, so hard cuts and OFFs don't click.
  • DEGRADE 10, CUTOFF C0: lo-fi, the hiss off, the breath kept.
  • Copies: 09 with PLAY REV (DRY B0, REV 60), every note a swell; 0C with DEL C0, REV 30, for throws into the delay.

Play it

Typed E-4 to A-4; with a C5 hum it sounds an octave up, E5 to A5, above the stabs. The same few pitches over different chords: over Am9 the long C is the third, over Dm9 the opening is the ninth, eleventh and fifth.

Try

  • A different vowel ("aah", "mm") as a second sample, swapped in on one bar.
  • If humming isn't for you: a Macrosynth VOWEL FOF holding a long "ooh", rendered and used the same way.
