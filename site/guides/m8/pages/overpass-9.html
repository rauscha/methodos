The voice

The hook is you: one hummed "ooh" on C, recorded on the M8's own mic, played back at other pitches as a melody. Short notes and hard cuts make it sound chopped, like fragments of a sung line rather than a singer. The backward copy swells in and cuts off. The instruments are on the Hummed voice card.

Draft choice.This page uses your hummed voice. If you'd rather not hum, the fallback is at the end: a Macrosynth VOWEL FOF "ooh" rendered to a sample and used the same way. Every phrase stays the same.
Listen forThe same line re-lit by the chord under it. Over Am9 the long C is the chord's third; over Dm9 the first bar's E, G, A are its ninth, eleventh and fifth. In bar 8, over Em9, the C would clash, so it drops to B. In the B section, the voice breaks into pieces, and some of them swell in backward.

The hook

Two bars, as it sounds with a C5 hum:

BarNotesRows
1E5 dotted eighth, E5 sixteenth (a stutter), eighth rest, G5 eighth, A5 quarter, eighth rest, G5 eighth0, 3, 6, 8, E; OFFs on 4 and C
2E5 dotted quarter, D5 eighth, C5 half0, 6, 8

A small climb to A and a fall past where it started, landing on C, the minor third. Pentatonic, syncopated on the "and"s: garage phrasing. Five pitches in all, so one hummed note pitched around stays natural.

Written as typed, an octave below as heard

The phrases say E-4, G-4, A-4, C-4. C-4 plays your hum at its own pitch, so if you hum the C as C5, in head voice, the hook sounds an octave above what's typed: E5 to A5, above the stabs. The roll draws the notes as typed. If C5 is out of reach, hum C4: the same phrases sound an octave lower, inside the stabs, which is muddier. Typing the hook an octave higher instead would pitch a C4 hum up an octave: thin, and likely past the pitch limit.

Record your C

A reference tone first, so you hum on C:

  1. A spare instrument, 0D: TYPE WAVSYNTH, SHAPE SINE, no mods (it holds for as long as the note does), DRY 80.
  2. A spare phrase 0D with C-5 on row 0, instrument 0D; a spare chain 0D holding it; song row 0F, track 1: chain 0D.
  3. Headphones on: the speaker would leak into the mic.

Record:

  1. Instrument 06: TYPE SAMPLER. EDIT on REC. opens the sample editor ready to record.
  2. SRC MIC. VOL: hum, and raise it until the loudest part sits high without clipping.
  3. PLAY: set it to row 0F, then EDIT on PLAY, so the song plays the reference tone.
  4. Start recording with the power button (no key click). Hum one "ooh" on the reference C, in head voice, breathy, for 3 or 4 seconds, and let it end softly: the soft end is where the backward copy will swell from. Stop.

How PLAY starts the song while recording. If it doesn't play row 0F, play row 0F in the song view, listen, stop, then record and hum from memory; DETUNE fixes the rest. Report back: how does PLAY in the sample editor start the song?

How a recording stops: EDIT again, or the power button (the field loop card asks too). Report back: what stopped it?

Edit:

  1. SELECT from the very start of the hum (zoom in with EDIT+LEFT/RIGHT) to about 1.8 seconds later, soft end included. PROCESS CROP. Why 1.8 s: the last backward swell (phrase 69, E-4, from beat 2) has three beats to reach the next downbeat, 1.45 s at 124 BPM, and E-4 plays the sample about a quarter faster than C-4.
  2. MONO: MIX (if it's stereo) and NORMALIZE. The voice plays up to A-4, nine semitones above C-4, so mono and 16-bit matter here; recordings are already 16-bit (changelog).
  3. A short fade at the very end, the last tenth of a second: FADE IN/OUT.
  4. NAME VOICE C. SAVE. OPTION leaves.

Tune and copy:

  1. With row 0F playing the reference, preview C-4 on 06 (EDIT+PLAY in the instrument view). Move DETUNE (80 is in tune; the second digit moves sixteenths of a semitone) until the two sound as one.
  2. Copy 06 to 09 (SHIFT+OPTION, OPTION+RIGHT, SHIFT+EDIT): PLAY REV, DRY B0, REV 60, NAME VOICE REV.
  3. Clear row 0F, chain 0D, phrase 0D and instrument 0D.

Instruments 06 and 09

  • PLAY FWD, LOOP ST. 00: every note starts at the front of the hum and doesn't loop; a long note ends with the hum.
  • DEGRADE 10, CUTOFF C0: a little lo-fi; the hiss off, the breath kept.
  • DRY C0, MFX 10, DEL 18, REV 40: close, a little echo in the gaps. EQ 06: a low cut, in step 10.
  • MOD 1, ADSR on VOLUME: ATK until the help line reads about 0.005 s, REL about 0.030 s, SUS FF. A tiny fade in and out, so the hard cuts don't click. ADSR's smallest values start around 2 ms (4.0 meetup).
  • 09: the same with PLAY REV, DRY B0 and REV 60: further away, and every note a swell.

The pitch limit. A-4 on a mono 16-bit sample should play; if it doesn't, DOWNSAMPLE it in the sample editor. Report back: does A-4 play?

Enter it

  1. Row 00, track 6: chain 60: 60 61 60 FD 60 61 60 62. The hook three and a half times: bar 4 is an OFF and silence, so the voice "only ever says half a phrase" at least once a loop.
  2. Row 01, track 6: chain 61: 66 FE 67 FE 66 FE 68 69, the B fragments.
  3. Phrase FD is an OFF on row 0; FE is empty.

The B fragments: backward on 09

  • 66 (Fmaj9): the opening, E, E, G, then the A on instrument 09: it swells in backward.
  • 67 (Gmaj9): a backward G through the bar, then a short forward A.
  • 68 (Gmaj9): G and A, forward, short.
  • 69: a backward E from beat 2, swelling into the breakdown's first downbeat, where the forward E answers it.

Where PLAY REV starts. Written as "from the end, backward", so each backward note swells for as long as the sample is. If phrase 69's E runs past the downbeat, lower 09's LEN; if 09 plays nothing, use a reversed file (09's sample editor: SELECT all, PROCESS REVERSE, NAME VOICE REV, SAVE as a new file, PLAY FWD). Report back: does the backward E end on the breakdown's first beat?

If you'd rather not hum: VOWEL FOF

  1. Instrument 0D as a MACROSYN, SHAPE 17 VOWEL FOF. TIMBRE about C0, toward "oo" (TIMBRE morphs a, e, i, o, u: manual); COLOR 40. MOD 1 an ADSR on VOLUME: ATK about 0.05 s, REL about 0.3 s, by the help line.
  2. Hold C-5 for two bars in a spare row, QUICK RENDER it (as in step 4), and edit and copy the render exactly as from "Edit" above.

The model's number: 17 is Braids' order in hex, unverified, as for the flute in Faded Polaroid. If 17 isn't VOWEL FOF, find it by name. Report back: what does SHAPE read at 17?
