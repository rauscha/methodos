Low Tide · step 4 of 10

The bassline

The roots from step 3 grow into a bassline. Same track, same instrument (02, the Roots card), new phrases. Rather than write the figure four times, you write it once in D and let the chain move it to the other roots.

The bass now moves inside each bar: the root, a quiet octave above it on the "and" of 2, a ghost of the root just before beat 4, the fifth on 4. Every two bars it slides down into the new root instead of jumping there.

The figure, in two phrases

Two bars per root, so two phrases: 24 for the first bar of each pair, 25 for the second. Both are written on D. Count the rows in sixteenths: row 0 is beat 1, 4 is beat 2, 8 is beat 3, C is beat 4.

  2. In phrase 24 and 25, enter the notes from the table, every one with instrument 02.
  3. On phrase 24 row 0, FX1: PSL 06. EDIT+UP on FX1 opens the FX help list.

Only root, octave and fifth. The chain transposes the figure chromatically, and those three are the same in every chord of this loop. A seventh or a third would come out wrong on one of them (C over D is a minor seventh; moved down to B♭ it becomes A♭, against the pad's A).

The chain moves it: TSP

The chain's second column, TSP, transposes a phrase in semitones for that row only. One pair of phrases then plays all four roots:

  • 00: D. FC: down 4 semitones, B♭. F9: down 7, G. FB: down 5, A.
  • Read the way the M8 reads its relative FX commands, values from 80 to FF count down from zero: FF is −1, FC is −4.
  • The roots land exactly where step 3 had them: D-2, A#1, G-1, A-1.

That TSP counts down the same way. The manual describes TSP only as "an optional note transpose in semitones"; FF-as-minus-one is how its relative commands work. Report back: what does the help line say for TSP FC?

The slide: PSL

PSL (pitch slide) glides into the note on its row from the note before, over a number of ticks. 06 is six ticks, one row. It's on row 0 of phrase 24 only, so the bass slides when a new root arrives (bars 1, 3, 5 and 7) and lands cleanly in the bar after. On 6.6.3 a retrigger resets PSL, so it doesn't leak into the rest of the bar.

The slides are big ones: from the fifth of one chord down to the next root, as much as a seventh. At six ticks it reads as a swoop, not a smear.

Mute T3 and play the bass alone. Bar 1: root, ghost, ghost, fifth. Bar 2, the same idea answered. Into bar 3, the swoop down to B♭. The ghosts should be felt more than heard; if they pop out, lower their V.

Things to try

  • PSL 0C: a slower, lazier slide.
  • TIMBRE 30 on instrument 02, so the bass speaks on a phone speaker (see the Roots card).
  • Delete the row B ghost and hear how much plainer the bar gets. In step 5 swing will push that row late, which is where most of the groove comes from.
